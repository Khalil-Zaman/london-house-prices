The Opportunity Cost

A median London home cost £235,000 in January 2006 and about £512,000 now — barely ahead of inflation, and flat for a decade. Put the same money in the S&P 500 instead and it would be £1.8m; in the NASDAQ-100, £5.5m. Pick your start month, switch to today's pounds, and watch the gap.

One pot of money, three destinies

Take the price of the median London home in your chosen start month. The claret line is what it buys you in bricks; the blue and bronze lines are the same pounds converted to dollars and put in the index, marked back to sterling every month.

Jan 2006

How to read it: the house line is the median price of the homes actually sold each month (bold = 12-month centred average, faint = raw months) — the mix of what sells shifts over time, so it is the market, not one house revalued. Index lines are price returns only: no dividends (reinvested, they would roughly double the S&P figure over twenty years), and the house line likewise earns no rent and pays no maintenance, stamp duty or fees. Dollars are converted at the spot rate each month. "Today's £" deflates everything by CPI to the latest month. The final ~6–12 months of the house series are incomplete (registration lag) and will revise upward.

Source: HM Land Registry Price Paid Data (2006 – May 2026, Greater London, standard sales); ONS CPI all-items index (D7BT); S&P 500 (^GSPC), NASDAQ-100 (^NDX) and GBP/USD via Yahoo Finance, monthly closes.
